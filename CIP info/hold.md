CIP — Core Premise (Second Generation)

Filing a Continuation-in-Part does not assert that an invention is correct.
It asserts that the invention is iterative.

A CIP creates a patent family, establishing continuity of authorship and disclosure across time. This introduces temporal layering: prior matter retains its original priority date, while new matter is anchored later.

The significance of this structure is procedural rather than substantive. It signals that the work is being refined within a coherent system, independent of outcome or eventual commercialization.

This distinction—between correctness and continuity—is foundational.
CIP — Core Premise (Second Generation)

Filing a Continuation-in-Part (CIP) does not assert that an invention is correct.
It asserts that the work is iterative.

A CIP establishes a patent family, creating continuity of authorship and disclosure across time. Prior matter retains its original priority date, while newly introduced matter is anchored at a later date. This produces temporal layering within a single invention lineage.

The significance of this structure is procedural, not substantive.
It signals sustained refinement within a coherent system, independent of outcome, commercialization, or eventual correctness.

This distinction — between correctness and continuity — is foundational.
